If I had to summarize one common thread linking my entire career journey, it would be Growth.

Since I started my career as a Software Engineer, I have been pursuing a vision of a Backend Engineer at the peak of his craft - from picking up the fundamentals of software engineering to designing planet-scale distributed systems. 9 years in and I find myself wanting to achieve more. I want to stand at the edge of knowledge and the frontier of technological innovation. I want to bring to life ideas found in research and academia.

For me, the purpose of this experiment is less about the vanity and the chase of a PhD title. If at the end of the day, I am able to conduct a proper review of academic literature, to extract and extrapolate the knowledge (explicit or otherwise), and to be able to bring forth novel ideas of my own, I'd consider that a success in my book.

But the journey will not be without its fair share of challenges. Admittedly, there is a gap between professional vs academic proficiency with the subject matter. I did not graduate from a CS program unlike most of my peers. In fact, I majored in Quantitative Finance and discovered my passion for Software Engineering later on.

And while my final year project was to write a thesis on Fast Fourier Transforms, that was 9 years ago and what I recall from that experience was a mixture of dread and feet-dragging. Perhaps a hubris of a young man, I hope that my older, more experienced self can go through that process with a lot more conviction. I guess that is the point of the experiment - will the pain be as sharp as I remember it to be or will the promise of the reward at the end be enough to move me.
