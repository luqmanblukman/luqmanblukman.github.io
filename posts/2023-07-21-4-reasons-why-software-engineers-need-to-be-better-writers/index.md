> AKA 4 Reasons Why I Have Decided To Write On Medium

![Photo by [Unseen Studio](https://unsplash.com/@craftedbygc?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)](image-1.png)

I'll have to be honest. I do not enjoy writing. In fact, it was one of the primary reasons I chose to pursue a degree in Mathematics. Working as a Software Engineer, I thought I could minimise (if not avoid) the amount of writing I had to do. And I am not alone in this. Many of the developers I have worked with share the same sentiment.

But as I progress in my career, I begin to have a growing appreciation for the people who dedicate their time to properly document the architecture of their system, the pain points that they are solving, what their code does, and more. In fact, I would even go so far as to say that writing is an essential skill for a successful career as a Software Engineer.

### 1. Good writing translates to good code

Uncle Bob has a quote that has stuck to me from the beginning of my career

> Clean code reads like well-written prose.

Working code communicates to the machine what you want it to do. Well-written code communicates to people what you intend for the machine to do. Well-written code is easy to follow and understand. Method names communicate sufficient information to other developers what it is doing. Methods are abstracted at the same level of abstraction. Complex logic is made simple to understand. Clean code isn't a jumbled heap of spaghetti code but a concise sequence of logical statements.

As for writing, a well-written article or document is easy to follow and understand. It understands its intended reader and communicates enough information to convey its message. It abstracts away information that is common knowledge. When needed, it is able to simplify complex information in a manner suitable for the person reading it. A well-written document requires organising your thoughts from a jumbled heap into a single coherent argument.

If that sounded familiar, that's exactly what's needed in writing clean code as well. There is no doubt in my mind that by becoming a better writer, you will get better at writing clean and legible code.

### 2. Documents outlive your time in the company

Here's a situation I hope you never find yourself in:

You and your team have been tasked to take over a system and its original developers have left the company. While the code itself is clean and easy to understand, there's no way of knowing why certain conventions were used or why a particular function has an obscure piece of logic in it which seemingly breaks away from the conventions established by the rest of the repo. Worst of all, there is no document explaining anything about the system.

![Photo by [Tim Gouw](https://unsplash.com/@punttim?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)](image-2.png)

It is not entirely outside the realm of possibility for such a scenario to occur. During hard times, entire teams and departments could get wiped out. But that does not mean that the systems they have built go with them. Instead, it falls on the employees that remain to maintain those systems and to understand its function.

Even if we were to dismiss such an extreme scenario, the reality is developers come and they go. The code that you write and the systems that you have built will in all likelihood outlive your time with the team/company. For the sake of the employees who remain behind, keep your documents up to date and make sure the next person who reads it understands everything they need to know about the system

### 3. Documents communicate the bigger picture faster

Which of this is faster?

- Reading 5 different repositories, figuring out how each one calls the other?
- Reading one single document laying out the architecture and topology of the 5 different services and how they relate to one another

I'm guessing you chose the latter. I would even go so far as to say that reading that one document will allow you to have a better appreciation of the code when you actually have to go through each repository.

The need for such a document happens surprisingly often --- initiating new joiners. New joiners are critically disadvantaged. They have no context of what has been done in the past. They have no idea of what you are doing now or what you are trying to achieve. They have no clues as to what the bigger picture is.

If you asked a new joiner to understand what the team does by reading the code, that would be like asking someone to understand the picture that jigsaw puzzle makes by studying each individual piece. By presenting them with the bigger picture, not only will they have a better idea of how things work, they also have a better idea of where they should start reading the code from.

### 4. Written articles amplifies your impact

As a junior developer, it is inevitable that I would spend most of my time coding and implementing features. But as I gained more experience, my scope of work began to expand. The size of the features I worked on grew and it eventually came to a point that I could not work on a single feature alone. That's where I learnt the value of writing.

![Photo by [ThisisEngineering RAEng](https://unsplash.com/@thisisengineering?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)](image-3.png)

If I only had my coding skills, I limited my impact to what I was able to do as a single person. Through writing, I am able to rally the skills and resources of multiple individuals. Through writing, I am able to communicate clearly what I want to achieve and how we can get there. Through writing, I am able to deliver more value to my organization than what I could have done on my own.

### Conclusion

I am still at the beginning of my adventures with writing. There is a lot more for me to learn with writing and Software Engineering. In times of doubt, I hope to look back at this and feel inspired to continue pressing forward. I hope that this article will do the same for you.
